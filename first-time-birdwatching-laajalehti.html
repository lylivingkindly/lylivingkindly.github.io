Published: 11/05/2025

Isn’t it ironic that the only bird I can identify is the artificial one — the bloody plane?

Having seen a random post on social media about this birdwatching session, hosted for free in English, I was sceptical at first — I remembered some events like that being used to buy foreigners into religious causes. However, eager to expand my horizontal point of view — literally — I gave it a shot. As expected, all jumping-out-of-comfort-zone decisions are mind-changing decisions.

I met the group at a tram stop. Once we gathered, we took a short walk to Laajalahden luonnonsuojelualue (Laajalahti Nature Reserve) to reach the birdwatching towers. In this session, the star species were waders, whose habitats are in wetland areas. We spent around one hour identifying and watching the birds, sharing experiences and discoveries, and chilling in the sun — probably just like some laid-back waders by the shore.

Birdwatching for beginners

Honestly, I had to ask someone in the group to show me how to use the binoculars I’d just borrowed, only to realise I was holding them upside down (LOL xD), so you can imagine how much of a novice I am. And in my deep respect, I could see a lot of planes in the air, though I had no single idea about the birds.

However, by the end of the session, I had managed to observe suokukko (ruff), punajalkaviklo (common redshank), and some kind of egret! I also learnt that the intrusive type of duck you might encounter on a city street this season is called valkoposkihanhi (barnacle goose). Having come across the töyhtöhyyppä (lapwing) a few times, I can confidently say I can now recognise them, especially by their distinctive sound. By great chance, we also saw one sääksi (osprey) flying overhead with a fish it had just caught — who says fish can’t fly?

It’s not just about birds

Along the way, I had a chat with a pair who were also joining, and it turned out they were mushroom experts! I asked some questions about mushrooms — what, how (and of course, where) to pick them. They said this is the season for a hazardous yet edible mushroom in Finland: it tastes good if you boil it twice and don’t (deeply) inhale the steam (don’t quote me on this — factcheck it yourself).

On the way back, they pointed out some true morels scattered seemingly out of nowhere. One of them said it was his first time seeing true morels (maybe this kartiohuhtasieni) in Finland. I think it’s a treasure too — imagine how easily I could have missed it if it hadn’t been for them.

It’s also about us

Apart from the thrilling sensation of trying something new, what made this trip so heartwarming was this group of people—all kind, intellectual strangers connected by a value that transcended hedonistic needs. They were respectful, supportive, and genuinely fun to be around. It’s strange (in a good way) that I didn’t feel lost among them. I felt like I was discovering and being myself in earnest.

And, last but not least, thank you so much, Sommer, for leading this group with your admirable accountability and kindness <3.
