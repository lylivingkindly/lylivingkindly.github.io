"Interview with an ape" - Felice Fallon

Published: 26/01/2025

If animals could speak, what would they tell us?

The world of animals unfolds through furry fingers, unspoken stories, furious anxiety, and collapsing respect. Einstein — the ape who knows how to sign — leads me into a world from which we’ve grown distant. It was sincere yet heartbreaking to feel the pain surfacing above words, sentences, and paragraphs. That is how I feel about this book.

Have you ever read a book written in memory of an animal? This book, "Interview with an ape", is dedicated to Sudan, the world’s last male northern white rhino, who died in 2018. He passed away, crushing the hope of saving the northern white rhino species from extinction — or, precisely speaking, from our own dangerous hands.

The book comprises different stories, and in each story, different angles — the animals and the human actors — thus weaving multiple perspectives into the storyline. Below are some of the conversations in the book that struck me with awe, pain, and a dawn of hope.

1. The little elephant thinking about the poacher

“I watch as he bends to touch my mother’s tusk. His smile is wide, revealing a gaping hole, and I wonder if other poachers have taken his teeth, and if so, how he’s managed to survive”.

2. The orca enslaved in the aquarium theatre

“I like that one day they’ll set me free, but her bosses (my note: the trainer’s bosses) will never allow this. We’re happy here, they tell the crowds. We teach them so much about our brethren in the wild. Hah! Do slaves teach their masters about freedom? I suppose in a way we do. And what is the lesson our masters learn?

Why, don’t be a slave, of course.”

3. The smart gorilla signing to Karim, a zoo staff member, about human love

“- So what is the problem?
- Those of us that love a human of the same sex in a sexual way as well as all the other ways, well, it frightens those that don’t. It frightens us when one of us is different.
- Does your love for Ali threaten human survival?
- What do you mean?
- Well, as you pointed out, two males can’t reproduce.
- Hardly. There are far too many of us as it is.
- I think if you love another human and that human loves you in return, this can’t be a bad thing.
Humans are very strange creatures.
- Yes, we are.”

4. Mrs Boss describing Mr Boss, a cattle farm owner

“Boss has been a hog farmer all his life, and his father before him, and before that his grandfather. Goes all the way back to his great-great-grandfather who was a pig farmer back in the old country. Ask Boss and he’ll tell you that for as long as he can remember he’s been surrounded by the smell of pigs: pig shit, pig piss, pig slop, pig fear, pig food. He hates what he does but he knows nothing else, and because he hates what he does he hates his pigs. Course he blames them for his unhappiness. I hate his pigs too, and because of it I’ve come to hate him. It’s a vicious cycle, and try as we might, we can’t break it. So he beats his pigs. And he beats me.”

5. Billy reflecting on his short but impactful time working for Boss

“The whole experience made me think long and hard about being a human. It was like a revelation. After that I thought about all the times I’ve spent lying on my back and staring up at the night sky – weather permitting – wondering if there’s intelligent life somewhere out there in the universe. And what for? Hell, we don’t gotta spend all that money sending people into outer space, building great telescopes and peering all over the galaxy looking for little green men. We only gotta open our eyes and look around us. There’s intelligent life everywhere. ‘Hiding in plain sight’, as Grandma likes to say.”

6. Karim and the smart gorilla signing about suffering

“- Allah is all powerful and he creates everything that exists. Sometimes he’s called God or Yahweh, but all humans just obey him regardless of what he’s called. We must do as he says at all times or we will suffer.
- But you are suffering now and you have suffered before, I have observed that all humans suffer. Does this mean that none of you obeys this allahgodyahweh of yours?
- We try.
- Perhaps you should try harder.
[...]
- Allah doesn’t expect animals to obey him. At least not in the same way.
- Then why do we suffer?
- I don’t know. It’s a mystery.”

7. Lastly, Dr Sadiq, the zoo’s researcher, and the smart gorilla signing for the last time

“- The poachers that killed your family have not gone away. Others have taken their place. This is a problem that will not end soon, and I’m afraid for you.
- And I am afraid for you. If you humans do not change your ways, your children will wake up one day and find their world emptied of all the abundant life that once thrived. They will pore over their picture books and read their stories and look around them and wonder what happened to this paradise you call Earth. And then they’ll look to you, their parents and grandparents, demanding answers you can’t give. Do not worry for me. Worry for them.”

And that is what animals would tell us if they could speak.
